# Surface brief - homepage

## Scope and mode

Public marketing homepage. Visitor mode: persuade. The visitor is evaluating whether cognilogs can take real work off a support team running any ticketing system.

## Audience, job, action

Support leaders and support-ops teams need to keep resolution times predictable without adding headcount. The page should earn a waitlist signup from teams who want end-to-end automation, not another reply suggester.

## Chosen direction

MausBot-pinned: paper-light surfaces, an oversized brand wordmark, a dark product console, detailed line-art support diagrams, and orange as cognilogs' own signal colour. The page borrows the reference's structural and illustration grammar, never its mascots, assets, or copy.

## Memorable moment

The confidence-threshold control: dragging the threshold moves a real reply between auto-send and human review. It makes the product's judgment visible rather than merely claiming it.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A support queue becomes a closed loop the visitor can see. Refuse the generic “AI support” hero of floating chat bubbles and feature cards.
OWN-WORLD: Cool paper ground, ink typography, orange signal accent, near-black product console; rounded 16–24px sheets; coloured ticket/intent nodes; hand-drawn dashed paths and small interface windows; no gradients or glows.
STORY: The visitor understands that one ticket event moves through intake, intent, evidence, confidence, handoff, and SLA watch; believes the loop closes; joins the waitlist.
FIRST VIEWPORT: Oversized orange “cognilogs” wordmark with floating support objects; black headline and one-sentence promise; two actions; the top of a dark support console rising from the fold.
FORM: Product-performs direction, redrawn for the pinned MausBot composition; interface fragments are built in HTML at readable size.
FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance.
